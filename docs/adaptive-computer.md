# Adaptive spatial computer

FileMapper's spatial computer uses the existing `SpatialGame` kernel for legal placements, whole-shape contact, scoring, and replay. A frozen challenge contains a deterministic piece sequence, evidence-backed patterns and declared geometry. Poses are proper rotations; they do not change source identity. Ordered lexical and path patterns require connected whole pieces whose X anchors increase in source order. Old pattern kinds retain their existing connected-set rule.

## Freeze and provenance

`filemapper computer freeze --request request.json --out challenge.json` freezes a cohort and challenge. The request provides absolute edition paths, selectedEdition, seed and protection registry. The optional `deckPolicy` with schema `filemapper.computer-deck-quotas/1` specifies wordLimit, pathLimit and candidateLimit; their sum may not exceed 128. The frozen deck summary reports actual and available counts and truncation reasons; unused quota is not refilled. Without deckPolicy, the legacy `pieces` field limits only words and path/candidate shapes can fill the 128-piece arena limit. Selection orders complete words by document and source offset, path components by source entry and path ordinal, then lexical candidates by source offset and kind. Candidates describe lexical observations; they are not parsed commands or evidence of execution.

Freeze hashes the editions, reads them under Windows read-sharing guards, rejects SQLite WAL, SHM and journal sidecars, and repeats the check before release. The protection registry lists known `validationEditions`, `testEditions`, and optional `trainingEditions`. Repository and revision families and exact source-content hashes are unioned across the full cohort and protected editions; a connected family crossing protected roles is rejected. The registry and edition commitments bind the cohort hash. This depends on the filesystem enforcing sharing rules and on an accurate supplied registry. It does not discover unlisted prior evaluations, forks outside the cohort, or copied content that differs at the byte level. Enlarging a cohort can change assignments, so checkpoints bind one frozen manifest. Never promote a previously exposed family to test by changing the seed or cohort.

`computer legal` returns the state, legal actions and orientations for a partial move sequence. `computer capture` builds an episode from a completed sequence and declared actor, session, model and checkpoint; `computer replay` verifies it. Each move records source occurrence, geometry and state hashes. Actor and reviewer identifiers are caller declarations, not provider authentication; preserve orchestration evidence separately. Verified reward excludes frozen learned-affinity advisory points and subtracts 1,000 for a top-out. No game result becomes a semantic supervision label.

## Reviewed expert policy

`computer expert-initial` creates the separate `filemapper.spatial-expert-policy/1` zero-weight checkpoint. `expert-train` accepts a frozen train-partition challenge, complete replay-verified episode, and an explicit review containing episodeHash, reviewerId, reviewId, rationale and reviewedAt. The review is an attestation, not an independently authenticated identity. The receipt records its review, exact episode, source cohort, parent and resulting checkpoints, candidate trace commitment, update attempts and actual weight-changing updates. `expert-verify` reproduces it.

The `spatial-outcome-features/1` ranker uses top-out, line and plane clears, evidenced pattern matches, landing height, resulting maximum board height, column cavities, and shape orientation footprint and height. The learner performs bounded deterministic pairwise imitation of reviewed choices, with clipped integer weights. It is not a semantic model and it has no proven improvement until evaluated independently. At inference it evaluates at most 64 evenly sampled legal placements. During training it also includes the reviewed placement if absent, so at most 65 are examined. Consequently an expert choice can be used as a training comparison even when that exact placement is outside the inference sample; the policy can generalize only through its features, and inference cannot choose an omitted move.

For continuation, `expert-train` accepts an ordered `lineage` of up to 31 prior entries, each containing `frozen`, `episode` and `receipt`; it replays every receipt in order from the known initial checkpoint and derives the next parent. `expert-compare` accepts up to 32 entries, rechecks the full chain, requires a test challenge in the same protected cohort, and reports its lineage hash and final checkpoint. It also accepts the single-update trainingFrozen/trainingEpisode/receipt form. Reusing a challenge plus identical ordered move trajectory is rejected even when actor or session labels differ. This does not detect near-duplicate trajectories, or similar material outside the manifest. A new review alone does not authorize reuse of an identical trajectory.

`expert-lineage-verify` accepts `frozen` and `lineage` and verifies up to 32 updates without training or requiring a test family. It returns the lineage hash, manifest hash and derived checkpoint. An empty lineage identifies the known initial checkpoint. `expert-play` can also derive its policy from this lineage; an optional supplied policy must match the derived checkpoint. Playing a supplied policy without a lineage remains available, but does not establish its training provenance. Reproducing a lineage does not measure generalization.

For the currently frozen 15-edition cohort under `expert-evaluation-v1`, known omnibrowser validation and Anton/mdkg training exposure are protected. Exact-content union leaves 14 train, one validation and **zero test families**. The balanced semanifest fixture has 16 words, one path component and eight lexical candidates under the 16/8/8 quota, with 14 ordered lexical patterns. It can support training demonstrations, not a held-out generalization claim. The older 128-piece candidate-flood fixture and prior failed held-out result remain historical artifacts.
